Management consulting has long rested on an elegant commercial architecture: the [[pyramid-model|pyramid model]]. A small partnership sells high-stakes advisory projects to corporate boards, while substantial cohorts of junior business analysts and associates execute the quantitative legwork. Clients paid premium hourly rates for junior teams to build financial models, survey market participants, pull industry benchmarks, and format presentation slides.

Generative artificial intelligence strikes directly at the base of that pyramid. When internal software tools can draft structured presentation outlines, synthesize hundreds of corporate earnings transcripts, and generate initial market models in minutes, the traditional justification for billing armies of junior analysts dissolves. ==Artificial intelligence is unbundling management consulting, forcing firms to shift from billing junior labor hours to selling verified judgment under outcome-based fee models.==

Navigating this transition requires understanding how cognitive automation reshapes advisory economics, delivery methods, and client negotiations.

## The idea, from zero

::: principle Build it from zero
1. A strategy consulting firm sells specialized problem-solving, strategic guidance, and analytical rigor to corporate clients.
2. Traditionally, the firm bundled two distinct activities into a single project fee: senior judgment (framing questions, managing client executives, identifying strategic risk) and junior execution (gathering data, summarizing filings, building slides).
3. The firm charged clients on a time-and-materials basis, generating high operating profit margins by billing junior labor at multiples of analyst compensation.
4. Foundation models automate the junior execution layer: drafting presentation decks, extracting table data, and writing initial spreadsheet routines at negligible marginal cost.
5. As clients recognize that software can produce first-draft deliverables, they resist paying premium hourly rates for routine analytical labor.
6. To preserve revenue and margins, consulting firms must decouple their fees from junior hours, adopting [[outcome-based-pricing|outcome-based pricing]] and deploying internal AI tools that enhance senior leverage.
7. ==Artificial intelligence changes the consulting revenue engine from a labor-arbitrage pyramid into a specialized judgment and technology pod.==
:::

## The empirical benchmark: BCG and the jagged frontier

Before debating business models, one must examine how artificial intelligence actually affects elite management consultants.

In March 2026, *Organization Science* published the finalized results of a landmark randomized controlled trial conducted by Fabrizio Dell'Acqua, Edward McFowland, Ethan Mollick, Hila Lifshitz-Assaf, Katherine Kellogg, Saran Rajendran, Lisa Krayer, François Candelon, and Karim Lakhani [@dellacqua-jagged-orgsci].

The researchers studied 758 elite management consultants at Boston Consulting Group (BCG), assigning them realistic business consulting tasks across two distinct performance domains:

::: stat +32 percent
The average improvement in output quality achieved by BCG consultants using artificial intelligence on tasks inside the technological frontier, alongside completing 12.2% more tasks and finishing 25.1% faster.
<span class="src">Source: Dell'Acqua et al., Organization Science (2026) [@dellacqua-jagged-orgsci]</span>
:::

The experiment produced two foundational findings that define the modern advisory landscape:
- **Inside the Frontier:** For creative product innovation, market positioning, and executive communication tasks that sat within the model's capabilities, consultants using AI completed **12.2% more tasks**, worked **25.1% more quickly** (reducing completion times by 22.5% and 27.6% across experimental tasks), and produced deliverables evaluated as **32% higher in average quality** [@dellacqua-jagged-orgsci]. The performance lift was largest among consultants who scored in the bottom half of the baseline distribution, compressing the internal skill gap.
- **Outside the Frontier:** The researchers introduced a business problem carefully constructed to sit outside the model's analytical frontier: a case with misleading surface data that required nuanced strategic deduction. On this task, consultants who used artificial intelligence were **19 percentage points less likely to produce a correct solution** compared to colleagues working without AI tools [@dellacqua-jagged-orgsci].

Consultants who trusted the model blindly suffered from automation bias: they accepted fluent, beautifully formatted explanations that were mathematically or logically incorrect.

This dynamic illustrates the [[jagged-frontier|jagged technological frontier]]. ==Within a consulting engagement, two tasks that appear equally complex to a human manager may sit on opposite sides of the machine's capability boundary.== An advisor who cannot judge where that boundary lies introduces severe operational liability into client work. Ethan Mollick, a co-author of the BCG study, observed that practitioners who navigate this frontier successfully adopt two distinct hybrid working styles: "Centaurs", who strictly partition tasks between human judgment and automated delegation, and "Cyborgs", who continuously integrate human and AI reasoning at every step of a workflow [@mollick-centaurs].

## Proprietary assistants and the agent workforce

Rather than waiting for consumer software to evolve, leading strategy firms built dedicated internal AI ecosystems.

At McKinsey & Company, leadership moved aggressively to deploy proprietary infrastructure across its global practice:
- **The Lilli Assistant:** In mid-2025, Fortune reported that over 75 percent of McKinsey's global workforce used the firm's proprietary generative assistant, named Lilli, on a monthly basis [@fortune-mckinsey-powerpoints]. Lilli synthesizes McKinsey's proprietary intellectual property, indexing decades of past project decks, market studies, and frameworks.
- **Questioning the Analyst Army:** Kate Smaje, McKinsey's global co-leader for technology and AI, observed: "Do we need armies of business analysts creating PowerPoints? No, the technology could do that" [@fortune-mckinsey-powerpoints]. The firm's total staff stood at approximately 40,000 professionals, down from more than 45,000 at the end of 2023 [@fortune-mckinsey-powerpoints].
- **A Fleet of 20,000 Software Agents:** In January 2026, McKinsey Global Managing Partner Bob Sternfels revealed on the Harvard Business Review IdeaCast that McKinsey had deployed a digital workforce of 20,000 specialized AI agents working alongside its 40,000 human consultants, taking on automated research and modeling workflows [@ft-mckinsey-lilli-interview].

At Boston Consulting Group, artificial intelligence transformed from an internal efficiency tool into the firm's primary commercial growth engine:
- **Revenue Acceleration:** In April 2026, BCG announced that its global revenue had expanded to $14.4 billion for 2025, up from $13.5 billion the prior year, marking 22 consecutive years of growth across 33,500 employees [@bcg-revenue-2025].
- **The Core Business:** Services focused on artificial intelligence and technology expanded by 25% year-over-year, coming to represent over 40 percent of BCG's total global revenue [@bcg-revenue-2025].

## The commercial shift: from hours to outcomes

==When client tasks can be drafted in minutes rather than days, selling consulting services by the hour becomes financially self-defeating.==

If a project team uses AI tools to complete a financial due diligence engagement in 200 hours instead of 800 hours, billing on a time-and-materials basis cuts the firm's revenue by 75%. To capture the economic surplus created by automation, consulting partnerships are fundamentally restructuring client contracts toward **[[outcome-based-pricing|outcome-based pricing]]**.

- **McKinsey's Global Move:** In a November 2025 financial briefing reported by Business Insider, McKinsey disclosed that approximately one-quarter (25%) of its global fee revenue was generated through outcome-based or performance-linked pricing arrangements [@bi-ai-fees-mbb]. UK Managing Partner Michael Birshan affirmed that the firm was actively shifting towards performance-based partnerships [@bi-ai-fees-mbb].
- **BCG's AI Engagements:** In May 2026, BCG Chief Executive Christoph Schweizer stated in the Wall Street Journal that for the majority (approximately three-quarters) of the firm's largest AI implementation cases, BCG operated under variable-fee or outcome-linked contracts [@wsj-bcg-schweizer-fees]. Across the firm's entire book of business, outcome-based pricing remained significantly below one-third. Traditional advisory retains its fixed-fee foundations, even as technology practices transition rapidly toward outcome billing [@wsj-bcg-schweizer-fees].

Under an outcome-based contract, a consulting firm bills directly for verified operational results: reducing client supply chain inventory by $50 million, shortening retail bank loan approval turnaround from seven days to twenty-four hours, or cutting customer care operating expenses by 20%.

If the consulting team hits the milestone ahead of schedule, the firm earns its full fee and a performance bonus, capturing the margin expansion enabled by software tools.

::: concept Outcome-based pricing
**Plain words.** Charging a client based on the measurable business result delivered (such as cost reduced, revenue gained, or cycle time compressed) rather than the number of consultant hours spent working.

**Analogy.** An architect who designs a bridge can charge by the hour for drawing blueprints, or negotiate a fixed percentage of the construction savings achieved through innovative engineering. The client cares about the bridge's safety and cost, not how many hours the draftsman held a pencil.

**Misconception.** "Outcome-based pricing means the consulting firm takes all the client's business risk." Effective outcome contracts define strict boundaries: fees link only to operational levers the consulting team directly controls or verifies, with explicit client data and resource commitments embedded as legal prerequisites.
:::

## The pyramid debate: hourglass or enduring structure?

How does cognitive automation affect the hiring and development of young consultants? The industry is divided into two distinct operating philosophies.

At major accounting and corporate audit networks (the Big Four), downward pressure on junior hiring has become visible:
- **Intake Reductions:** In June 2025, HR Review documented significant contractions in UK graduate and school-leaver intakes across the major multidisciplinary professional networks: KPMG reduced graduate intake from 1,399 to 942 (a 29% decline), Deloitte reduced intake from 1,700 to 1,400 (down 18%), EY fell 11% to 1,600, and PwC declined 6% to 1,500 [@hrreview-bigfour-intake].
- **Structural Reshaping:** In December 2025, the Financial Times reported that two senior Big Four executives estimated that UK large-firm graduate recruitment would be down by about a half in the coming year, with starting salaries freezing after years of expansion [@ft-pyramid-salary-freeze]. Management theorists described a shift from the traditional "pyramid" to an "hourglass" or "obelisk": a narrow junior tier for prompt auditing, a thin mid-level, and a dense senior specialist layer [@ft-pyramid-salary-freeze].

By contrast, the leadership of elite strategy partnerships rejects the premise that entry-level consultants are obsolete:
- **BCG's Talent Intake:** Christoph Schweizer maintained in the Wall Street Journal that BCG had not reduced its junior hiring cohort [@wsj-bcg-schweizer-fees]. The firm received 1.7 million applications globally in 2025, admitting fewer than 1 percent into its ranks [@wsj-bcg-schweizer-fees].
- **The Talent Argument:** Strategy firm partners emphasize that junior analysts do not exist merely to produce slides. They are the firm's future partners in training. If a partnership cuts its junior intake to boost short-term profitability, it eliminates the apprenticeship pipeline required to produce skilled engagement managers five years later.

Furthermore, strategy firms face intense new competitive threats. In an August 2025 investigative briefing titled *Is McKinsey losing its crown to AI?*, The Economist analyzed the changing landscape [@economist-mckinsey-crown]:
- **Slowing Historical Growth:** After doubling revenue between 2012 and 2024, McKinsey's top-line revenue growth slowed to roughly 2% annually, with estimated headcount declining by about 5,000 professionals from peak levels at the end of 2023 [@economist-mckinsey-crown].
- **New Technology Competitors:** Companies like Palantir and specialized foundation model labs increasingly deploy forward-deployed software engineers directly into client executive suites [@economist-mckinsey-crown].
- **Unbundling Cogitation from Grunt Work:** The Economist observed that elite consulting historically bundled profound strategic insight with junior data-gathering. As software bots automate data-gathering, corporate clients question why they should pay top-tier rates for the entire bundle, threatening fee compression across the industry [@economist-mckinsey-crown].

::: case Illustrative · The Mumbai Turnaround Fee-at-Risk Negotiation
#### The situation
A Mumbai-based management consulting firm with 200 analytical consultants pitches an engagement to a major Indian retail bank with 1,200 branches.

The bank's managing director wants to compress the credit evaluation and sanctioning turnaround time for micro, small, and medium enterprise (MSME) loans from fourteen business days to forty-eight hours.

The consulting team estimates that an engagement deploying retrieval-augmented LLM assistants for financial statement spreading, automated bank statement cross-referencing, and credit memo drafting will require four months of work.

Under traditional time-and-materials billing, the project would cost ₹4.5 crore (staffed by one Partner, one Engagement Manager, and four Associates).

The bank's chief financial officer counters with an aggressive proposal:
- The bank will pay a fixed base retainer of ₹2.0 crore.
- The remaining ₹2.5 crore (plus an upside bonus of ₹1.5 crore) will be placed strictly "at risk," payable only if the average loan approval turnaround across all 1,200 branches drops below 48 hours within six months of deployment.

#### The decision
The consulting engagement partner conducts a risk audit of the client's operational workflow:
1. **Control of Operational Levers:** The consulting team controls the deployment of the AI tool, the prompt architecture, and the automated spreading algorithms. However, final loan approvals require physical sign-offs from regional credit committee branch managers. If branch managers resist the software or delay opening their review queues, turnaround will exceed 48 hours regardless of software performance.
2. **Data Availability:** Automated financial analysis requires normalized tax filing (GST) and banking data feeds. In 30% of rural and semi-urban branches, applicants submit scanned paper account statements with poor optical clarity.
3. **Downstream Bottlenecks:** While the AI tool drafts the credit appraisal memo in thirty minutes, the legal verification of mortgaged property titles takes an average of six days and involves municipal land registries outside the bank's digital perimeter.

The partner restructures the commercial contract into a hybrid risk-sharing model:
- **Base Fee:** ₹2.5 crore to cover core technology infrastructure integration and direct operating expenses.
- **Operational Milestone 1 (₹1.5 crore at risk):** Linked strictly to the cycle time of steps the consulting team directly designs: reducing financial statement analysis, credit memo drafting, and risk classification time from four days to four hours for all digital loan files.
- **Business Milestone 2 (₹1.5 crore upside bonus):** Linked to overall branch turnaround falling below 72 hours, conditional upon the bank enforcing a mandatory 24-hour service-level agreement (SLA) on regional credit committee reviews.
- **Data Exclusion Clause:** Loan files requiring manual physical property title deed searches or illegible scanned documents are tracked under a separate turnaround category and excluded from the SLA bonus calculation.

The restructured contract was accepted by the bank board. The project achieved its operational milestones in month five, earning the consulting firm ₹5.5 crore in total fees while delivering a 65% reduction in MSME loan processing latency for the bank.

#### Your move
==When negotiating an outcome-based consulting contract, never tie your firm's fees to an aggregate business metric influenced by factors outside your direct operational control.== Disaggregate the client's process: tie fee-at-risk milestones to the automated stages you engineer, and establish explicit client operational SLAs for human sign-offs.
:::

## Where this shows up at work

::: lens consulting
For aspiring strategy and management consultants, the transformation of the consulting firm redefines the skills required to survive and advance. The era of building a consulting career solely on fast Excel financial modeling and PowerPoint slide formatting has closed. Partners look for associates who operate comfortably inside the jagged frontier: candidates who can orchestrate software agents, challenge model outputs with rigorous primary research, conduct empathetic stakeholder interviews, and frame unstructured corporate dilemmas for board-level decision-makers.
:::

::: interview The outcome-based consulting proposal
**Q.** A prospective retail banking client asks your consulting firm to undertake a digital transformation engagement on a pure outcome-based fee structure: linking 100% of your firm's compensation to a 40% reduction in retail loan approval turnaround time. How would you evaluate this proposal, and what terms would you negotiate before signing?

> "I would advise against accepting a 100% outcome-based contract on loan turnaround time without contractual safeguards. While outcome pricing aligns incentives and can yield high margins, loan turnaround is governed by factors outside our team's control, such as client branch behavior, credit committee meeting frequency, and legal title checks. I would restructure the engagement into a hybrid model that separates automated process efficiency from client-dependent operational stages."

**What they are testing.** Whether you understand the economic mechanics of management consulting, know how to manage operational and commercial risk, recognize client organizational dependencies, and can structure balanced professional service agreements.

**A strong structure.**
1. **Audit Operational Control:** Disaggregate the loan origination value chain. Separate tasks directly influenced by the consulting intervention (credit memo drafting, automated document parsing, algorithmic risk scoring) from tasks controlled by bank personnel (credit committee approvals, customer interview scheduling, external property verification).
2. **Evaluate Baseline and Data Integrity:** Verify that historical turnaround time is accurately measured. If the client's baseline data is flawed or inconsistent across branches, proving that a 40% reduction occurred becomes legally contentious.
3. **Negotiate a Hybrid Financial Structure:**
   - Establish a base fee to cover core delivery costs and technology licensing expenses.
   - Tie the variable fee-at-risk component to specific operational milestones directly engineered by the consulting team (such as reducing credit assessment drafting time from 8 hours to 1 hour).
   - Establish client service-level agreements (SLAs): bank credit officers must review and act on generated memos within 24 hours for the final turnaround target to apply.
4. **Risk Protections and Governance:** Include exclusions for edge cases: applications with incomplete documentation, non-standard corporate structures, or municipal property registry delays must be excluded from the turnaround calculation.
:::
