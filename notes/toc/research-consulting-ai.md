## 1. Research on consultants using AI

- [A] Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier, Dell'Acqua, McFowland, Mollick, Lifshitz-Assaf, Kellogg, Rajendran, Krayer, Candelon, Lakhani. HBS WP 24-013, Sept 2023; published Organization Science, Mar-Apr 2026 (DOI 10.1287/orsc.2025.21838). https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700 . Preregistered, 758 BCG consultants, 3 arms (no AI / GPT-4 / GPT-4 + prompt-engineering overview). 18 tasks inside the frontier: AI users completed 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% more quickly, "significantly improved quality". One task outside the frontier: AI users "19% less likely to produce correct solutions". (✓)
- [A] Same paper, journal text, https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838 . Exact: quality up "by an average of 32%" (Section 4.1); outside the frontier "an average decrease of 19 percentage points" (Section 4.2). 97.6% of treated subjects used AI at least once. Bottom-half-skill subjects were "the most significant beneficiaries", top half "also received a significant boost". (✓) **Flag: the brief's "quality +40%" is not what the published version says; use 32%. The 19 is percentage points (the HBS abstract says "19%", loosely).** Working-paper-era figures 43% (bottom half) and 17% (top half) seen only in a secondary write-up; not found in the journal text. (~)
- [A] The Cybernetic Teammate, Dell'Acqua, Ayoubi, Lifshitz, Sadun, E. Mollick, L. Mollick, Han, Goldman, Nair, Taub, Lakhani. HBS WP 25-043, Mar 2025; Organization Science, Jul-Aug 2026. https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=67197 . 776 P&G professionals, preregistered, AI vs none x individual vs two-person team. "Individuals with AI matched the performance of teams without AI"; AI "breaks down functional silos"; more positive emotion with AI. (✓)
- [A] The Cybernetic Teammate (author summary), Ethan Mollick, One Useful Thing, 22 Mar 2025. https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-cybernetic-teammate . Teams beat individuals by 0.24 SD without AI; individual with AI +0.37 SD; team with AI +0.39 SD (not significantly different from individual with AI); AI groups saved 12-16% of time; AI teams more likely to produce top-10% solutions; participants had 10+ years at P&G. Author's own caveat: GPT-4, so possibly a lower bound. (✓)
- 2026 follow-ups by these authors on consultants: none found. The 2026 events are the journal publications above. (~, searched only)

## What this means for the consulting track

Candidate chapter claims, each backed above. Claim wording is for discussion, not final.

1. **AI helps most inside its frontier and hurts outside it, and consultants cannot tell where the edge is.** 758 BCG consultants: +12.2% tasks, 25.1% faster, quality +32% inside; 19 percentage points less often correct on the one task outside (Dell'Acqua et al., Org Science 2026). Also: bottom-half performers gained most. Backs a chapter on "judgment over output" and a pitfalls exercise.
2. **One person with AI can do what a two-person team did, and the boundary between specialisms blurs.** P&G 776-person trial: individual+AI matched team without AI (+0.37 SD vs +0.24 SD team-only); R&D and commercial people produced balanced ideas (Mollick summary, HBS WP). Caveat: product ideas, GPT-4.
3. **The product is moving from advice to outcomes, and fees are following.** Sternfels "migrating... to much more of an outcomes-based model" (FT); McKinsey ~25% outcome-based (BI); BCG 3/4 of largest AI cases variable-fee but under a third of all work (WSJ). Backs a business-model chapter and a case on how to price an AI project.
4. **Consulting firms are now AI vendors as well as advisers.** BCG: AI-and-tech work over 40% of revenue, AI services +25% (press release); Accenture: more than 400 clients began advanced AI in FY26 (call summary ~), but its release gives no AI revenue and consulting revenue grew only 3% in local currency; McKinsey 20,000 agents against 40,000 people. Teach students to read these claims as self-reported.
5. **The pyramid is under pressure, but the evidence of fewer juniors is mixed and mostly Big Four.** Down: UK Big Four intake (KPMG -29%, Deloitte -18%), PwC -5,600 global, salary freeze third year, anonymous executives expecting UK graduate hiring down about half. Not down: BCG "not reduced our junior intake", Bain graduates +25%, McKinsey NA +12%. Firms' own explanation is largely a weak market (Amitrano) with AI anticipation as a second factor. A chapter should say this plainly and avoid "AI is killing entry-level jobs".
6. **What the entry-level job becomes: supervising and challenging AI output.** KPMG "managers of agents"; McKinsey's Lilli interview tests whether you can "challenge it, put it into context"; Smaje on "armies of business analysts creating PowerPoints". Backs an AI-fluency-for-case-interviews chapter: prompt, verify, then judge.
7. **Hiring criteria are shifting toward judgment and non-obvious thinking, with AI fluency assumed.** Sternfels on liberal arts, failure and "discontinuous leaps"; BCG "not the output of a tool"; Accenture recruiter on digital literacy. Candidate chapter: what a student can show that a model cannot.
8. **India needs its own thread, and the evidence is thin.** Campus consulting share is high (IIMB 42%, IIMC ~40% of 2024 batch), and Deloitte India talks about upskilling ~30,000 and hiring "similar profiles", but no firm primary source on India AI practices or an India AI interview exists in this pass. Keep India claims as signal until the Mint originals and placement reports are read.

### Corrections to the brief's numbers
- Jagged frontier quality: published figure is 32%, not 40%; outside-frontier drop is 19 percentage points (journal text).
- McKinsey agents: 20,000 on the IdeaCast (6 Jan 2026), not 25,000.
- BCG: AI-and-tech over 40% of revenue; 25% is AI services growth (BCG release), not revenue share.
- "Pricing shift": McKinsey ~25% outcome-based is a press-briefing figure, not an annual-report figure.
