### AACSB
- [A] AACSB, "A Framework for Artificial Intelligence in Business Education: July 2026 Update", Tawnya Means, 28 Jul 2026, 84 schools. Read through NotebookLM notebook a9cb0efc (grounded answer, citing the source). The aacsb.edu URL I guessed 404'd; the notebook holds the text. (✓ via NotebookLM)
  - Four shifts: awareness to **literacy** ("AI literacy is increasingly viewed as a foundational competency"); infrastructure to **governance**; adoption to partnerships; teaching ethics to **embedding responsible AI**.
  - Eight themes include democratisation ("AI literacy across all learning pathways, not only specialized tracks") and domain-specific applications.
  - Learning goals shift "from producing outputs to interpreting, critiquing, and improving those outputs."
  - A related AACSB audit example splits an Analyst track (prompting, data analytics, code generation) from a Manager track (AI governance, organisational impact, decisions under uncertainty, "human-AI co-intelligence").
  - Cited number: AI "able to handle 50-60% of typical junior employee tasks"; so "frame problems, ask good questions".
  - Competencies listed: problem framing; critical interrogation of output (hallucination, bias, omission); responsible AI, privacy, IP, regulation; visible judgment and decision ownership; human-AI collaboration; domain application; communication and defence.

## 3. Order the curricula use

- HBS: AI today (landscape + strategic use) -> data & inference -> ML and AI factories (incl. ethics/regulation) -> strategy and implementation -> build an agent.
- MIT exec: intro -> ML -> gen AI/NLP -> robotics -> business & society -> future.
- Google: intro -> productivity tools -> prompting -> responsible AI -> staying ahead.
- Anthropic: Delegation (what to hand to AI) -> Description (prompting) -> Discernment (judge output) -> Diligence (ethics, accountability). The loop Description <-> Discernment is iterative.
- Yale: imperative -> vibe coding -> RAG -> agents -> tokenomics (build-first, concepts via building).
- INSEAD: taxonomy -> which AI for which problem -> posture of scepticism.
- Elements of AI: understand (Part 1) -> build (Part 2, code).

Two patterns: **(a) concept-first** (HBS, MIT, Kellogg, EoAI): landscape, then data/ML, then LLMs, then strategy, ethics last or woven; **(b) behaviour-first** (Anthropic, Google, Mollick, Karpathy "How I use"): use it, prompt it, judge it, be responsible. Yale and the Kellogg ops section are **(c) build-first**. Almost everyone puts ethics/responsibility late or as a thread, and strategy/work last.

## 4. Dependency chain (what must be understood before what)

1. **Kinds of AI** (rules vs learned; prediction vs generation). Needed so "AI" stops meaning "ChatGPT" (INSEAD roadmap; MIT, Kellogg).
2. **Learning from data** (training, a model is a function fitted to examples, error, overfitting at the idea level). Needed before anything about quality or bias.
3. **Next-token prediction, tokens, context window** (Karpathy, 3B1B, Anthropic capabilities). Needed for: hallucination, cost, prompting, RAG.
4. **Hallucination and jaggedness** (follows from 3, plus training data). Needed before evaluation.
5. **Cost = tokens x price x calls** (follows from 3). Needed before agents (loops multiply cost) and ROI.
6. **Prompting as briefing/delegation** (needs 3-4: the model only has what is in the context).
7. **Grounding/RAG** (needs 3 and 6: add documents to context; needs 4: why).
8. **Tools and agents** (needs 6-7 plus 5: a loop of model calls with tools; error compounds).
9. **Evaluation and judgment** (needs 4 and 8: how do you know it works; human in the loop; AACSB's "interpret, critique, defend").
10. **Business layer**: stack/economics (needs 5), adoption and ROI (needs 8-9), risk/ethics/regulation (needs 2, 4), work and jobs (needs 8).

Ethics is not at the end of the chain: bias needs only step 2, privacy needs 7. Most curricula treat it as a thread.

## 6. Findings that would change the table of contents

1. **Add a "kinds of AI" opener.** INSEAD calls it nonnegotiable; 7 of 12 sources start there; the draft jumps straight to LLMs.
2. **Weave ethics/responsible AI through every Part I chapter**, not just ch10; it is the single most common topic (9/12) and AACSB's "embed responsible AI, not standalone ethics" says so directly.
3. **Make "judge and defend AI output" the book's spine.** AACSB, Anthropic's Discernment, INSEAD, Kellogg, Mollick and the McKinsey Lilli evidence in DISCUSSION.md agree; it is where the draft's thesis and the canon meet.
4. **Decide the "no-code build" question**: the freshest required courses (Yale, HBS, Kellogg ops) have managers build agents; the draft forbids code. Zero-code does not forbid no-code.
5. **Cost/tokens and the AI stack are real differentiators**, not canon; the book should know it is choosing to depart.
