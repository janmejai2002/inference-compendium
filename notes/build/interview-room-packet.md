## Interview questions (provenance: JD = our JD scan; CB = student casebook pattern [signal]; MBB = firm statement. None claimed as asked at a named firm.)
**Definitional**
1. In two sentences, how does generative AI differ from the analytics a bank already uses? (CB: GenAI for text vs ML for numbers; JD: Accenture "fundamentals of AI, GenAI, and Agentic AI concepts")
2. What is an AI agent and how is it different from a chatbot? (JD: "agents" is the top word in AI-role JDs, 54%)
3. What is a hallucination, and when is it a risk you cannot accept? (JD: Merck "recognize its limitations")
4. Why can the same prompt give different answers, and what does that mean for a client process? (JD: Bain "quality control")
5. What separates an AI pilot from production, and why do many stall? (JD: change management 42%)

**Judgement**
6. Here is an AI-drafted case structure. What is missing and what do you check first? (MBB: Lilli pilot "review its output, and apply judgment")
7. An AI tool gives a market size in one second. How do you decide whether to trust it? (JD: Bain "strong judgment and quality control")
8. Tell me about a time AI got something wrong for you and how you caught it. (JD: Vantrock "show what you have done with them")
9. Would you paste client data into a public chatbot? What would you do instead? (JD: governance asks; privacy thread)
10. How would you explain to a client why an AI recommendation should or should not be followed? (JD: Merck "ethical judgment around fairness, privacy, and accountability")

**Case-shaped (CB)**
11. A retailer wants GenAI for recommendations, forecasting and service. Where is value highest and where do you start? (CB: Indian-retail GenAI Q&A)
12. Convince a reluctant client to adopt AI in KYC, claims or credit memos. Pitch and objections? (CB: "convince the client to adopt AI")
13. A large firm offers to buy an AI start-up. How do you value it and tell real AI from a label? (CB: AI due-diligence cases)
14. A call centre wants to replace agents with a chatbot. What do you measure before and after? (CB: call-centre chatbot cases)
15. Estimate daily prompts to a ChatGPT-style app in India. (CB: the one recurring AI guesstimate)
16. Prioritise ten automation ideas for a manufacturer. (CB: impact-feasibility matrix)

**GD and ethics**
17. "AI will cut entry-level consulting jobs." Argue both sides with evidence. (research-consulting-ai: evidence mixed)
18. Should an employer use AI to screen candidates? (Deloitte 72%; BCG "never make decisions")
19. Who is accountable when an AI recommendation harms a customer: vendor, firm or analyst? (accountability thread)
20. Is it fair to deny a loan on an AI score the customer cannot see? (CB: AI-based telco score case; DPDP/RBI FREE-AI angle)

## Indian business example
Illustrative (no real firm): a mid-size NBFC gets 3,000 applications for 40 analyst seats and a vendor tool ranks CVs by keyword match. A candidate who writes "built a loan-default dashboard" ranks below one who writes "credit risk analytics, SQL" for the same work. Lesson: mirror the JD's words, expect a human only after the machine. Anchored on the Deloitte 72% fact; no invented numbers.

## Misconceptions
1. "Using AI to prepare is cheating." Firms differ: BCG allows brainstorming and practice; Bain London bans AI in assessments and interviews.
2. "AI decides who gets hired." BCG and McKinsey both say humans decide and McKinsey says no one is rejected solely on its AI interview; the screen still shapes who is seen.
3. "The AI interview tests whether you know AI." The reported pilot tests whether you can prompt, review and judge.
Stat block: **72%** of 257 surveyed organisations use AI for resume screening (Deloitte India, Aug 2026).
