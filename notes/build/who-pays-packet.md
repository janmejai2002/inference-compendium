## Interview questions
1. "A private-equity fund is considering a Rs 5,000 crore stake in an Indian data-centre operator. How would you decide whether AI demand justifies the price?" Pattern: M&A / due diligence (casebook research: 6 of 12 core AI cases are M&A/DD; PwC data-centre cost drivers case; Deloitte data-centre-to-cloud case). Strong answer: contracted vs speculative tenants, cost stack (power, land, chips, cooling), utilisation, power access, customer concentration.
2. "A large Indian bank spends Rs 40 crore a year on its own servers. Should it move to cloud or buy AI as an API?" Pattern: Deloitte "move two private data centres to cloud" and "100 on-prem apps to cloud" cases; Accenture AI-pitch cases (research-casebooks-2025-26.md). Strong answer: capex to opex, falling cost per query, data residency, lock-in, exit cost.
3. "A consumer-electronics giant weighs buying an AI-assistant company for $500M. Who pays for the compute behind it, and how does that change your offer?" Pattern: "AI Enterprise Acquisition" case #70 (IIM-A, M&A, Challenging). Strong answer: cloud dependency, gross margin after inference cost, whether token prices keep falling, dependence on one model provider.

## Misconceptions (myth blocks)
1. "AI is cheap now, so the buildout is a waste." Unit price fell 280-fold (2025 Index) while total spend soared (facts 1-4). Cheaper units raise usage; the bill is volume times price.
2. "Hyperscalers pay for the boom from profits." Alphabet's quarterly capex exceeded operating cash flow, Meta's nearly equalled it, Amazon's trailing free cash flow was -$42.4B, Oracle carries about $125B of debt (facts 1, 3, 4, 5).
3. "Revenue growth proves it pays off." Lab revenue is $19-25B annualized (Stanford, early 2026) to $47B+ (Anthropic, May 2026), against single funding rounds of $65B and $122B. Revenue is not profit; compute is the big cost line (Stanford ch.4, figure 4.2.20).

## Stat for the stat block
**$51.4 billion**: Alphabet's capital spending in one quarter (Q3 2026), against $42.3B of operating cash flow (fact 1). Alternative: 29.6 GW of AI data-centre power by Q4 2025 (Stanford).
